# A157250 — Wilson numbers: search log (started 2026-10-04)

n ≥ 1 is a **Wilson number** if P(n) + e(n) ≡ 0 (mod n²), where P(n) = ∏_{1≤a≤n, gcd(a,n)=1} a
and e(n) = +1 if n has a primitive root (n = 1, 2, 4, p^k, 2p^k with p odd), e(n) = −1 otherwise
(Gauss: P(n) ≡ −e(n) (mod n)). Equivalently n divides the generalised Wilson quotient
A157249(n) = (P(n) + e(n))/n.

**State of the art before this work.** Agoh, Dilcher, Skula, *Wilson quotients for composite
moduli*, Math. Comp. 67 (1998) 843–861, Table 1: 13 Wilson numbers ≤ 5·10⁸:
1, 5, 13, 563, 5971, 558771, 1964215, 8121909, 12326713, 23025711, 26921605, 341569806,
399292158 — exactly the current OEIS data. Heuristic (ADS, repeated in OEIS): the count below N
is about (6/π²)·ln N.

## Method

### Prime-power decomposition (derived here, numerically verified)

For p^k ∥ n write n = p^k·m, G_k = ∏_{j<p^k, p∤j} j (Gauss factorial),
A(m) = ∏_{ℓ|m prime} ℓ^{−φ(m)/(ℓ−1)}. Then

    P(n) ≡ A(m)^{φ(p^k)} · G_k^{φ(m)}   (mod p^{2k})   for every prime power except 2¹,
    P(2m) ≡ A(m) · (−1)^{φ(m)/2}         (mod 4)        for odd m ≥ 3.

Proof sketch: Möbius inversion over d | m turns P(n) into ∏_{d|m}(d^{Mφ(p^k)} ∏_{b≤p^k M, p∤b} b)^{μ(d)}
with M = m/d; the inner product is ∏_{c<M} ∏_{j<p^k, p∤j}(c p^k + j) ≡ G_k^M (mod p^{2k}) because
∑_{j<p^k, p∤j} 1/j ≡ 0 (mod p^k) (generalised Wolstenholme; it holds for p = 3 and for 2^k with
k ≥ 2, and fails only for 2¹, hence the special case); ∏_{d|m} d^{μ(d)m/d} = A(m) is the identity
∑_{d|m} μ(d)(m/d) v_ℓ(d) = −φ(m)/(ℓ−1). Verified against the direct product for every
prime-power component of every n ≤ 3000 (6845 components, 0 mismatches), and the derived
Wilson test agrees with a direct scan of all n ≤ 200000 (`runs/wq_5e8/brute_scan_2e5.txt`) and
with 3000 random n ≤ 3·10⁷ (the `-check` option).

Writing G_k = ε + p^k·w (ε = −1 for odd p and for 2², +1 for 2^k, k ≥ 3; w = generalised
Wilson quotient, w = w_p = ((p−1)!+1)/p mod p for k = 1) and using additivity of the generalised
Fermat quotient q(x) = (x^{φ(p^k)} − 1)/p^k mod p^k:

    P(n) ≡ ε^{φ(m)} · (1 + p^k·(ε·φ(m)·w − t)),   t = ∑_{ℓ|m} (φ(m)/(ℓ−1)) · q(ℓ)   (mod p^k).

So the p^k-component of "P(n) ≡ −e(n)" is a congruence mod p^k needing only w(p^k), φ(m), the
primes ℓ | m and q(ℓ). n is a Wilson number iff all components pass.

### Pipeline (`tools/`, all local to this Mac so far)

1. **Wilson quotients w_p for all primes p ≤ N/2** with the Costa–Gerbicz–Harvey/Gerbicz program
   `pw13` (class e = 2 covers every prime at these sizes), patched to write `wilsonres.bin` =
   (p, w) uint64 pairs (w ∈ [1, p]; w = p marks a Wilson prime). Eight single-threaded processes:
   * 7 … 2.5·10⁸: 13,679,315 primes, 100 s wall (`runs/wq_5e8/`)
   * 2.5·10⁸ … 2³¹−1: 91,418,247 primes, 30 min wall (`runs/wq_2p32/`)
   Spot checks against `tools/wilson_brute` (exact 128-bit (p−1)! mod p²) at 10⁶, 4·10¹¹ and
   three primes near 2³¹ all agree; `wilsonnum` also verifies that the table has every prime.
2. **`tools/wilsonnum.c`** (pthreads): sieves primes to N/2 and smallest prime factors to N/3;
   computes w(p^k) for k ≥ 2 by direct Gauss-factorial products; then visits every n ≤ N exactly
   once at its largest prime power P^k (loop over P, then over m ≤ N/P^k with P ∤ m and all prime
   factors of m below P), evaluates the P-component with a per-thread cache of Fermat quotients,
   and fully tests the survivors. N < 2³² keeps every modulus p^{2k} ≤ N² inside 64-bit
   Montgomery arithmetic. Throughput: 4.9·10⁸ n in ≈30 s on 8 threads (plus ≈6 min of w(p²)
   precomputation at N = 5·10⁸).
3. **Verification of hits**: `tools/wilsonnum_brute` (direct, C, threads), `tools/wilson_verify.py`
   (direct, Python), and `wilsonnum -n <n>` (prints each component).

### Validation history (keep — it matters for trusting the output)

* First 5·10⁸ run reproduced 12 of the 13 known numbers and two of the new ones but **missed
  341569806 and 139459265**. Cause: after a full test, the Fermat-quotient cache stamp coincided with entries
  written for the candidate's last prime, so later m under the same P read wrong quotients.
  Fixed (stamp bumped after every full test); `-n 341569806` now passes all five components, and
  the rerun (`runs/wq_5e8/wilsonnum_selftest_5e8_v2.log`) finds exactly the 13 known numbers plus
  the three new ones, with 0 mismatches on 3000 random n ≤ 3·10⁷ plus the known terms against
  the direct definition.
* The pw13 table stores w = ((p−1)!+1)/p ∈ [1, p], not reduced mod p; `wilsonnum` reduces it.
* The 64-bit Montgomery reduction originally formed t + m·n in 128 bits, which overflows for
  moduli above ≈1.1·10¹⁹, i.e. p⁴ with p > 58000. It only affected w(p²) for 58000 < p ≤ 65535
  (relevant to n = p² alone, never a Wilson number without a Wilson prime), but it was fixed
  before the final 2³² run. w(p²) is now computed in O(p) from the power sums of 1/i mod p⁴
  (Newton identities, e₁,e₂,e₃) and the block expansion G₂ = ((p−1)!)^p ∏_c (1 + cpe₁ + c²p²e₂ + c³p³e₃);
  the program checks this against direct products for all 5 ≤ p ≤ 1500 and p = 4099, 16411,
  60013, 65521 at every start, and both agree with exact values for p ≤ 23.

## Cost beyond the Mac

At N = 2³² the Mac does everything in under an hour. Scaling: the quotient table costs
≈ 50 µs per prime here (Stage 2 of pw13 grows like log³ p; at 4·10¹¹ it was 73 µs per prime on a
20-thread Spark), so N = 10¹² needs w_p for 2·10¹⁰ primes ≈ 1–2 weeks on both Sparks, and
`wilsonnum` must switch to segmented factoring (spf to N/3 no longer fits), 128-bit moduli, and
the O(p) symmetric-function method for w(p²) (direct products are Σp² ≈ 2·10¹⁶ at p ≤ 10⁶).
Expected new terms: (6/π²)·ln(N/5·10⁸) ≈ 1.3 at 2³², 3.2 at 10¹¹, 4.6 at 10¹².

### Structure of the new terms

All but two new terms are "lifts" p^a·W of known terms W. For the k = 1 components this is
automatic: the ℓ-component condition for p^a·m differs from that of p·m only by the invertible
factor p^{a−1} (through φ), so only the p-adic component is new — a single extra congruence
mod p^{a−1}·(…). For W = 341569806 = 2·3·181·409·769 and 399292158 = 2·3·17·97·40357 the
cofactors have so many factors of 2 and 3 in φ(m)/(ℓ−1) that the 2-adic and 3-adic conditions
for 2^a·3^b·W are satisfied identically for small a, b (2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12 times W all appear;
9·399292158 does not, because 40356 contributes only 3² and the 9-component fails). The 1998
paper proved the necessary direction (Props. 3.1, 3.2) and therefore searched only squarefree
moduli, missing these. The genuinely unrelated new terms are 2340914226 = 2·3·7·55736053 and
3623403714 = 2·3·7·86271517 (both 42·p).

The heuristic count (6/π²)·ln N ≈ 13.5 at 2³² badly undercounts because of the lifts;
it was fitted to squarefree-only data.
